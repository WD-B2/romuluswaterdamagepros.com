When standing water is an emergency.

If water is pooling on your floor right now, treat it as an emergency and call the 24/7 line. While you wait, stop the source if it is safe, keep power off in wet rooms unless the panel is dry and reachable, and lift furniture legs and valuables out of the water.

Water moves quickly into baseboards, carpet pad and the subfloor beneath. Extraction is the step that stops that spread, which is why it comes before any drying equipment goes in.

After hours and weekends are when many of these calls arrive, because burst lines and failed sump pumps do not keep business hours. Same-day response is what we work toward when crews are available, and the person on the phone will tell you what to expect for your address.

What water extraction involves, step by step.

  1. Safety check.

    We look for electrical hazards, slip risks and signs the water is contaminated before anyone enters the room.

  2. Source and spread.

    We confirm the source is stopped, then map where the water traveled using moisture meters and thermal imaging.

  3. Pump and extract.

    Submersible pumps clear deep water and truck-mounted or portable extractors pull it from carpet, pad and hard floors.

  4. Remove what cannot dry.

    Saturated pad, damaged baseboard and soaked insulation come out so they do not hold water against the structure.

Clean, gray or black water: why the category changes the plan.

Water is sorted into three categories in the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard that many restoration professionals use as a reference. Category 1 comes from a clean source such as a supply line, category 2 carries some contamination such as a washing machine overflow, and category 3 is grossly contaminated, such as sewage or floodwater.

The category decides how much has to be removed. Clean water may let us save carpet and pad, while contaminated water usually means porous materials are discarded. If the water came up through a drain, read our page on sewage backup cleanup before you touch anything.

What affects the cost of water extraction.

Cost factors, no surprises.

Price depends on how much water there is, how many rooms it reached, which materials are wet and how long it sat. Category matters too, since contaminated water adds protective steps and disposal.

We explain scope and price before work starts, and the written scope shows what was extracted and what was removed.

Insurance and documentation.

Many homeowner policies cover sudden water events but not every cause, so call your insurer early and describe the loss as water damage. We photograph the water, the affected rooms and the removed materials, and our scope lists the work so your adjuster can review it. The coverage decision stays with your insurer.

Where extraction fits with drying and repair.

Extraction removes the bulk of the water, but the structure still holds moisture afterward. Most jobs move straight into structural drying, and a pipe failure may also need the burst pipe cleanup steps. After a heavy rain the flood damage restoration page covers what comes in from outside.
