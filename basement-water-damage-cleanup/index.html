Water in the basement: what to do now.

Basement flooding is an emergency, and our 24/7 line is open for it. Do not go down if water is near outlets or the panel, keep the power off in the flooded area unless you can reach the panel dry and call so pumping can start.

If you can do so from a dry spot, stop the source, whether that is a line or an appliance. If it is rain or groundwater, move valuables upstairs and photograph the room from the stairs.

These calls come at all hours, especially during storms. Same-day response is what we work toward when crews are available, and the person on the phone will tell you what to expect.

If your basement has flooded before, ask your insurer about water or sewer back-up endorsements and check the FEMA flood map for your address.

How we clean up a basement.

  1. Pump and extract.

    Submersible pumps remove depth and extractors take water from carpet and low areas.

  2. Remove wet materials.

    Pad, bottom drywall, insulation and stored items that cannot dry are removed.

  3. Clean and treat.

    Surfaces are cleaned with an antimicrobial where needed, especially after contaminated water.

  4. Dry.

    Dehumidifiers and air movers run with daily readings until the basement is dry.

Finished versus unfinished basements.

Many Southeast Michigan homes have basements, and finishes vary widely. A bare concrete floor dries fast, while finished walls, carpet and insulation hold water and need removal.

We ask about your layout before we plan so we bring the right equipment.

What to do with stored belongings.

Basements hold boxes, tools, holiday items and sometimes furniture. Lift what you can onto shelves or upstairs, and photograph anything that was wet before moving it, since your insurer may want to see it.

Paper, photos and fabric dry best if handled quickly. Photos, papers and fabric dry best if handled quickly, so move them to a dry place and keep them flat or spread out.

Sump pumps, floor drains and window wells.

Three features account for many basement problems. A sump pump moves groundwater out of a pit, a floor drain carries water to the sewer, and a window well keeps soil away from a basement window.

If the pump fails, the drain backs up or the well fills, water has nowhere to go but in. Testing the pump, keeping drains clear and covering wells are simple steps that reduce risk.

What affects the cost.

Cost factors.

Cost follows depth, finished area, contamination and drying time. A finished basement costs more than an unfinished one because there are more materials to remove and replace.
