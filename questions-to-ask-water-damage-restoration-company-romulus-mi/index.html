What to Ask a Water Damage Restoration Company Before You Hire in Romulus, MI.

In an emergency it is hard to compare companies. These questions help you tell a clear plan from a vague promise.

Call (844) 950-6900

By the Romulus Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 2026.

Why the questions matter.

Water damage calls are made under stress, often at night, and companies know it. A few direct questions help you separate those who explain their work from those who just want to start.

Good answers are specific and in writing. Bad answers dodge price, scope or documentation.

Red flags on the phone.

  • Pressure to sign right now before you can read anything.
  • A price with no scope behind it.
  • Promises about what your insurer will pay.
  • Refusal to explain how drying will be measured.
  • A claim that every home needs the same equipment package.

What a good first call includes.

A person asks where the water came from, how far it spread, whether power or sewage is involved and what the floors and walls are made of. They tell you what to do while you wait and say plainly what they cannot know until they see the house.

If you want to see what that sounds like, our water extraction page and structural drying page describe the steps and the readings we use.

How to compare two written scopes.

When you have two scopes in hand, line them up room by room. Look for the same rooms, the same materials and the same drying approach, then see where they differ.

A scope that removes more material is not automatically better or worse. Ask why, and ask what readings support the choice. A company that cannot explain a line item probably cannot defend it to your insurer either.

Also compare what is excluded. If one scope leaves out disposal, equipment days or contents handling, the lower number may not be lower once everything is counted.

Timing questions that are fair to ask.

It is reasonable to ask when someone can start, how many days equipment is expected to run and when you will hear about progress. A good company gives a range and explains what could change it.

Be careful with any company that promises an exact arrival time during a storm, because crews cannot control traffic, closures or the number of calls in the queue.

What the company will ask from you.

Expect to share your address, the source of the water and access to the home. You may be asked to sign a work authorization, which should match the scope you were given. Read it before you sign.

You will also be asked about your insurer, because the company needs to know whether to document for a claim. That does not mean the company will bill the insurer for you or promise payment.

Questions about the rebuild.

Ask whether the company does the repair work or only the drying, and whether the rebuild is priced separately. Some homeowners prefer a single team, and others prefer to hire their own contractor for finishes.

If another contractor will rebuild, ask the drying company for the moisture logs, since they show the structure was dry before walls were closed.

Romulus note: standards worth knowing.

Romulus note: what to verify.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 is a published industry standard for professional water damage restoration, and many professionals use it as a reference for water categories and drying goals. It is a document, not a credential, so ask any company how it applies the standard to your job.

For local context, the National Weather Service recorded 3.31 inches of rain in Romulus in about an hour and a half on July 28, 2025. After storms, many homes call at once, and it is fair to ask any company honestly how long reaching you will take.

Choosing between a local and a distant company.

A company based near you can usually reach you sooner and knows local conditions. Ask where the crews start from, and for towns around Romulus, check whether the company has pages for places like Inkster or Garden City, which suggests it covers them.
