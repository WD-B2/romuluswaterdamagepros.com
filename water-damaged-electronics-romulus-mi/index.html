What Water Damage Does to Electronics and When to Unplug in Romulus, MI.

Water and electricity do not mix, and the first minutes decide whether a device can be saved. Here is what to do.

Call (844) 950-6900

By the Romulus Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 2026.

Safety before saving the device.

If water is near outlets, power strips or the panel, do not reach into it to unplug something. Turn off the breaker for that area only if you can reach the panel without standing in water, and call an electrician or your utility if you are unsure.

Once the area is safe, unplug dry devices and move them out of the water. Never plug in a device that was wet, even if it seems to work.

Why drying a device is not the same as safe.

Water carries minerals and, if the source was floodwater or a drain, contaminants. When it dries on circuit boards it leaves residue that can cause corrosion and short circuits days or weeks later.

A device that powers on after a soak may fail later, which is why repair technicians usually clean boards rather than rely on drying alone.

When to repair and when to replace.

For a phone or laptop with important data, a repair shop may be able to recover it even if the device is not worth fixing. For a TV or small appliance that sat in floodwater, replacement is usually safer than repair.

Keep receipts and photos, since many policies cover contents. Our guide to appliance leak water damage explains how leaks from machines start.

How to describe damaged devices to your insurer.

List each device with its brand, model, age and approximate value, and attach photos of the label and of the water damage. If you have receipts or order emails, save them to the claim folder. Electronics are often lost in basement floods, so note the water depth when you list what was affected.

Contents coverage often has limits for categories such as electronics, so ask your agent how your policy handles them. Keep the damaged units until the insurer tells you to dispose of them.

Data recovery basics.

The data on a device is often worth more than the hardware. If a phone, laptop or drive holds irreplaceable files, power it down and take it to a data recovery or repair specialist as soon as possible rather than experimenting.

A technician can open the device, clean the board and copy the files to a working drive. Delays and attempts to power on can lower the chance of recovery.

Dealing with a wet outlet or cord.

Do not use an outlet or extension cord that got wet until an electrician checks it. Water inside an outlet box can create a shock or fire risk even after it dries.

If a breaker trips repeatedly after water exposure, leave it off and call an electrician. Do not keep resetting it.

A quick safety recap.

Make the area safe first, power down second and let a professional look at anything valuable. Never test a wet device by plugging it in.

Keep ruined items and photos until your insurer says otherwise, and save receipts for any replacements.

Why the published standard does not cover electronics repair.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard covers professional water damage restoration of buildings and contents handling, but it does not make a wet device safe to turn on. A repair technician or the maker decides that.

Citing the standard describes an industry document and not a certificate. Keep the device off and unplugged until it is checked.

Protecting electronics next time.

Keep electronics off the floor in basements, use surge protectors and consider a battery backup for key equipment. Store backups of important data off site and in the cloud.

If your home floods often, ask an electrician about raising outlets and the panel where code allows. For flooded basements in New Boston or Belleville, our crews can pump out and dry the space while you handle the devices.

What not to do with wet devices.

  • Do not use a hair dryer or oven, since heat can warp parts.
  • Do not charge a wet phone or laptop.
  • Do not shake a device with water inside, since it spreads the water.
  • Do not rely on rice, which has little proven benefit.
  • Do not pour alcohol on live circuits without guidance.
