Belleville Lake and the water problems around it.

Belleville sits on the south shore of Belleville Lake, a lake of about 1,200 acres that stretches roughly six miles and is the largest inland lake in Wayne County. It was formed when Detroit Edison built the French Landing Dam on the Huron River in Van Buren Township in the mid 1920s, and the city of about 4,000 residents is surrounded by Van Buren Township.

For a homeowner that geography has practical meaning. Lakeside lots, shoreline subdivisions such as Doane's Landing and homes near downtown on Main Street sit close to water, so groundwater, storm runoff and seepage into lower levels are real concerns. Check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurer what is and is not covered.

How the seasons change water risk by the lake.

Spring brings snowmelt and heavy rain, which raise groundwater and push water against foundations. Summer storms can dump a great deal at once, and the National Weather Service recorded 3.31 inches in nearby Romulus in about an hour and a half on July 28, 2025.

Winter adds frozen pipes in garages and exterior walls, and the Detroit area averages about 45 inches of snow a year in the 1991 to 2020 normals. Each season needs a different checklist, which is why we ask about the home before we plan the job.

Insurance and documentation in Belleville.

Michigan DIFS says flood damage is not covered by a standard homeowners policy, and that limited water or sewer back-up coverage can be added by endorsement. Because Belleville is lakeside, ask your agent about flood coverage before you need it.

When you call your insurer after a loss, describe it as water damage, take photos and keep damaged items until the insurer says you can discard them. We provide a written scope and moisture readings for your adjuster.
