Wall and Drywall Water Damage Repair in Romulus, MI.

Wet drywall softens, stains and peels. We find the moisture, remove what failed and rebuild the wall so it stays dry.

How to tell a wall has taken water.

Stains, bubbling paint, a soft or crumbling surface, warped baseboards and a musty smell all suggest water. A wall can look fine while the insulation inside is wet, which is why meters and thermal cameras are used.

Dry in place or cut it out.

Drywall that was wet briefly with clean water can sometimes dry in place, especially if we vent the cavity. Drywall that is soft, stained deeply or wet with contaminated water is cut out.

Insulation is treated the same way. Wet batts do not dry well and are usually removed.

How we repair a wet wall.

  1. Locate.

    Moisture mapping shows where water is and where it stopped.

  2. Open.

    Baseboards come off and drywall is cut above the wet line in clean sections.

  3. Dry.

    Air movers and dehumidifiers dry studs and plates, then we verify with readings.

  4. Rebuild.

    New drywall is hung, taped, textured and painted to match.

Why the source matters first.

A new wall will stain again if the leak is still active. Whether it is a supply line, a drain or a roof, the source has to be fixed first. See burst pipe water damage for line failures and ceiling water damage repair when water comes from above.

When this is urgent.

Active wetting is urgent and the 24/7 line takes those calls. A dried stain can wait a few days, but the cause should be found soon.

What is inside a wet wall.

A typical interior wall has drywall on both faces, wood or metal studs, sometimes insulation and wiring or pipes. Water that enters at the top runs down inside, and water that enters at the base wicks upward into the board by capillary action.

That is why a stain a foot above the floor can mean wet drywall two feet up, and why we measure beyond the visible mark. We follow readings until they match dry reference.

Common ways walls get wet.

  • A supply line or fitting inside the wall that weeps or splits.
  • A shower or tub surround that has lost its seal.
  • A roof or window leak that runs down the inside face.
  • An appliance or fixture next to the wall that overflowed.
  • Groundwater that wicks up from a damp slab or basement floor.

Painting, texture and finishing after drying.

Once the wall is dry we tape and finish the new seams, prime the patch and match texture as closely as the existing finish allows. Primer matters because new drywall is more absorbent than old paint and will show as a flat spot if left unprimed.

If the damage reaches a corner or a long wall, we may paint the whole face so the color is even. We discuss that choice in the scope so it is no surprise.

Industry standard and wet walls.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for professional water damage restoration, and it helps decide when drywall can be dried and when it must be cut out. Water category and time are the two main factors.

We cite the standard as a guide to our method. It is not a claim that we hold a certificate.

What affects the cost.

Cost factors.

Cost follows the wall area, drywall thickness, how much insulation is removed and the finish, such as texture or wallpaper. Matching paint and texture on an existing wall takes extra care.

Insurance and related services.

Photograph the wall before repairs and describe the loss as water damage when you call your insurer. Drywall repair works best when drying comes first, as covered in structural drying.
