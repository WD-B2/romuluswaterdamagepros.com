The water problem New Boston homes have most: the basement.

In New Boston, the most common call is a wet basement. The Huron River runs through the community and the Waltz Road bridge crosses it just south of the central business district, so groundwater and storm runoff are never far away.

Basements here take water from heavy rain, a failed sump pump, a floor drain that backs up or a supply line that lets go. Each has a different fix, and the first job is working out which one you have.

Our basement water damage cleanup page explains the process, and structural drying covers the step that follows.

How your home's age changes the plan.

Older homes tend to have older plumbing, older sump systems and more finishes that have been added over time. Newer homes may have more finished basements, which means more drywall and carpet to remove when water arrives.

We ask about the age of the house, whether the basement is finished and where the sump pit is, because those answers decide equipment and crew size. A bare concrete floor dries quickly, while carpeted, finished space holds water for days.

Rivers, parks and getting around New Boston.

New Boston is an unincorporated community in Huron Charter Township. It is near Lower Huron, Oakwoods and Willow Metroparks, I-275 runs through it and Detroit Metropolitan Airport sits just to the north in Romulus. The community was first settled in 1827, and its name changed from Catville to New Boston on March 20, 1868.

Because New Boston is part of a township, ask the township about permit rules before a rebuild. Our base in Romulus is about six miles north, and we will tell you plainly how long reaching you is likely to take.
