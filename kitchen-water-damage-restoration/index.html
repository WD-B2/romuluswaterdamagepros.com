Kitchen Water Damage Restoration in Romulus, MI.

Kitchens have more water lines than any other room. We dry cabinets, flooring and walls after a leak and rebuild what cannot be saved.

Why kitchen leaks do so much damage.

A kitchen packs a sink, a dishwasher, often a refrigerator ice line and sometimes a disposal into a small space. A leak under any of them runs into cabinet bases, toe kicks and flooring, then spreads across the room.

Cabinets are the hidden cost. Particleboard boxes swell when wet and do not shrink back, so fast drying decides whether you replace one section or the whole run.

How we dry and rebuild a kitchen.

  1. Find and stop.

    We confirm the source is shut off and note it for your plumber.

  2. Pull toe kicks and open cavities.

    Lifting trim and drilling vents lets air reach wet areas behind cabinets.

  3. Dry.

    Air movers and dehumidifiers dry cabinets, flooring and wall bottoms.

  4. Repair.

    Swollen cabinet sections, flooring and drywall are replaced if they do not recover.

Which materials can be saved.

Solid wood cabinets and tile floors usually dry well. Particleboard and laminate that stayed wet more than a day often do not. Hardwood flooring depends on how fast drying started, and our hardwood floor page explains the choices.

We tell you which items we expect to save and which to replace, using moisture readings rather than guesses.

Timing and urgency.

A running line is urgent and the 24/7 line takes those calls, but a slow leak found under the sink can often be planned for the next day. Call either way, because moisture left under cabinets keeps doing damage.

What to do in the first hour at home.

Shut off the valve for the source, empty the cabinet under the sink and set out towels, then call. Take photos of the cabinet floor, the wall and the flooring before you move anything.

Do not run the dishwasher or disposal until the leak is fixed. Running water through a bad line adds to the damage.

Countertops, appliances and what stays in place.

Granite, quartz and laminate tops rarely need removal, but the cabinet below them can. If the cabinet has to come out, the countertop may need to be lifted, and that is a scope item we discuss before it happens.

Appliances such as the dishwasher and refrigerator are pulled so we can reach the floor beneath. We reconnect them only after the floor is dry and a plumber confirms the source is fixed.

Industry standard for kitchen water losses.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for professional water damage restoration. In a kitchen it helps decide whether cabinet bases and subfloor can be dried or must be removed, based on the water source and how long it stood.

We cite it as an industry document that guides our process. It is not a claim that we hold a certificate.

What affects the cost.

Cost factors.

Cost follows how many cabinets and how much flooring were wet, how long the leak ran and the finish level of the kitchen. Custom cabinets cost more to replace than stock ones.

We explain scope and price before work starts.

Insurance and related services.

Insurers differ on sudden and gradual leaks, so ask about yours and describe the loss as water damage. Photograph the source, the cabinets and the floor. See appliance leak water damage for dishwasher and fridge failures and structural drying for the drying step.
