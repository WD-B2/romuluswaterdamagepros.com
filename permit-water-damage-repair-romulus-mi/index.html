Permits for Water Damage Repair in Romulus, MI: Who to Ask.

The answer depends on what is being repaired and where you live. Here is how to find out before work starts.

Why the answer is local.

Permit rules in Michigan are enforced locally, so the place to ask is the building department for your city or township. A drying job rarely needs one on its own, but repairs that touch structure, wiring or plumbing often do.

We do not quote permit requirements from memory, because rules change and vary between communities. The most reliable move is a short phone call to the building department that covers your address.

How to ask the building department.

  1. Have your address ready.

    The department needs to know which jurisdiction you are in.

  2. Describe the work.

    Say what was damaged and what will be repaired, in plain terms.

  3. Ask what is required.

    Ask about permits, inspections and who may pull them.

  4. Write it down.

    Note the date, the name and the answer in your claim folder.

Romulus, New Boston and the rest: different offices.

Romulus is a city, so its building department handles permits within city limits. New Boston is an unincorporated community inside Huron Charter Township, so questions there go to the township, and nearby cities such as Belleville, Inkster and Garden City each have their own offices.

If you are not sure which jurisdiction you are in, start with your property tax bill or the county, and ask when you call. Our pages for New Boston and Belleville explain the local setting.

Who pulls the permit.

Often the contractor doing the work pulls the permit, and homeowners can sometimes pull permits for their own home with limits. Ask the building department who may apply for each type of permit.

Be cautious of any contractor who suggests skipping permits to save time. Unpermitted work can cause problems with insurers and at resale.

Mistakes that cause trouble with permits.

  • Closing walls before a required inspection.
  • Hiring someone who says permits are unnecessary without checking.
  • Assuming a permit from a neighbor's job applies to yours.
  • Not keeping the final inspection record.
  • Starting work before the permit is issued.

Questions to ask your contractor about permits.

Ask who is responsible for permits, whether permit fees are in the price and who schedules inspections. A contractor who has handled permits in your community can usually tell you what the building department asks for.

If your insurer is paying for repairs, ask whether the claim requires proof of permits. Having that answer early avoids delays near the end of the job.

Romulus note: local facts.

Romulus became a city in 1970 and covers about 36 square miles, according to Census figures, and permit questions for properties inside the city belong with the City of Romulus building department. Call the city directly for current requirements instead of relying on a list on a website.

The National Weather Service recorded 3.31 inches of rain in Romulus in about an hour and a half on July 28, 2025. After storms like that, building departments can be busy, so ask early if you expect repairs that need a permit.

Permits and insurance.

Insurers may ask whether repairs were permitted and inspected, so keep copies of permits and final inspection records. If an inspector finds a problem, fix it before you close up walls.
