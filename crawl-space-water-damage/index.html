Water under the house, out of sight.

A crawl space is a low, enclosed area under part of a house. Water gets in from heavy rain, a high water table, a leaking line or a failed sump pump, and it often sits unnoticed until the floor above feels soft or smells damp.

Not every Romulus home has a crawl space, since many have full basements, and some are built on slabs. If yours does, it is one of the places we check first after a storm.

How we clean and dry a crawl space.

  1. Pump the water.

    Small pumps remove standing water from the low points.

  2. Remove ruined material.

    Wet batt insulation, loose debris and saturated sections of the vapor barrier come out.

  3. Dry the space.

    Dehumidifiers and air movers lower humidity and dry joists and subfloor underside.

  4. Check the source.

    We note drainage or plumbing problems so a plumber or contractor can fix them.

Why moisture under the floor matters upstairs.

Wet joists and subfloor can warp flooring above, make floors feel soft and raise humidity in the living space. Hardwood is especially sensitive to moisture from below.

If you notice cupping or a musty smell, check the crawl space before you replace the floor, because the new floor will fail the same way. Our hardwood floor page covers the floor itself.

When it is urgent and when it can be scheduled.

A flooded crawl space after a storm or from a running pipe is urgent, and the 24/7 line takes those calls. A slow seep that appears in wet seasons usually allows time to plan, but wet insulation keeps working against the floor, so do not wait weeks.

Signs that water is under the floor.

  • A musty smell that comes up through floor vents or near the access hatch.
  • Floors that feel soft, spongy or uneven in one area.
  • Condensation on pipes, ducts or the underside of the floor.
  • Pest activity that increases after wet weather.
  • A higher indoor humidity reading than usual.

Why drainage and the vapor barrier matter.

A vapor barrier is a plastic sheet that covers the soil and limits evaporation into the space. When it is torn, missing or bunched, ground moisture rises more easily and the crawl space stays damp even without a leak.

Cleanup resets the space, but long term dryness depends on drainage and the barrier. We point out what we see so a contractor can quote the fix.

Planning around a tight space.

Crawl spaces are low and confined, so work takes longer and equipment is smaller. Crews wear protective gear and check air and wiring before entering.

If the access hatch is blocked by shelving or stored items, moving them ahead of time speeds things up.

The published standard and crawl space moisture.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for professional water damage restoration, and ANSI/IICRC S520 covers mold remediation. We name both because a wet crawl space can involve either, and each document has its own purpose.

Citing them describes industry references we use as a guide. It is not a statement that we hold a certificate, and we make no health claims about moisture.

What affects the cost.

Cost factors.

Cost follows crawl space size, water depth, how much insulation and vapor barrier needs replacing and how hard the space is to access. Tight, low spaces take longer to work.

We explain scope and price before work starts.

Insurance and related services.

Whether insurance applies depends on the cause, so ask your insurer and describe the loss as water damage. Groundwater and rain seepage are treated differently from a plumbing leak. For heavier water see flood damage restoration, and for the drying step see structural drying.
