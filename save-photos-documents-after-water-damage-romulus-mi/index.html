How to Save Photos and Important Documents After Water Damage in Romulus, MI.

Photos and papers cannot be replaced, so the first hours matter. Here is what to do and what to avoid.

Call (844) 950-6900

By the Romulus Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 2026.

Act fast and handle gently.

Wet paper and photographs begin to stick, warp and grow stains within a day or two. Speed matters more than perfection, so move them to a clean, dry place as soon as it is safe to enter the room.

Handle items by the edges, with clean hands, and do not try to separate stuck pages by force. That tears emulsion and ink.

What to do, step by step.

  1. Sort by urgency.

    Separate irreplaceable items such as photos, wedding records and legal papers from everyday paperwork.

  2. Remove loose dirt.

    Gently rinse mud or debris with clean cool water, without rubbing the surface.

  3. Lay flat or hang to dry.

    Spread items on clean paper towels or hang with clips, away from sunlight and heat.

  4. Freeze if you cannot dry in time.

    Seal items in freezer bags and freeze them to buy time until you can dry or hire a specialist.

Digital copies and backups.

Once items are dry, scan or photograph them so you have a copy. Store a copy in the cloud and on a drive kept outside the house, because the next leak could hit the first backup.

For legal documents such as deeds, titles or insurance papers, contact the issuing office for replacements and keep a list of what you need.

Handling muddy or contaminated papers.

If photos or papers were soaked in floodwater or sewage, wear gloves, avoid touching your face and work outdoors or in a ventilated area. Rinse with clean water gently, then dry as above. Wet paper and photos often come from burst pipe losses, so the source matters when you describe the event.

Do not use household bleach on photos or paper, since it can strip color and damage the fibers. If the items are valuable, a specialist can clean and stabilize them with better methods than a home rinse.

A short list of records to rescue first.

  • Birth, marriage and military records.
  • Deeds, titles, mortgage papers and insurance policies.
  • Tax returns and financial statements.
  • Medical records and prescriptions.
  • Family photographs, negatives and slides.

Digitizing as you dry.

Take a quick phone photo of each item before you start cleaning, even if it is damaged. If the original deteriorates, the image may be the only record you have.

Label each photo with the date and what it shows, and store the images in the cloud right away.

Asking for help with a big collection.

If a large collection of photos or papers is wet, ask relatives and friends to help sort and dry in shifts. Set up tables in a dry, ventilated room, and give each helper a clear task.

Do not rush. A calm system saves more items than a panicked scramble, and freezing gives you time to organize.

Storing papers safely next time.

Use plastic bins with lids, store them above floor level and avoid basements and attics where temperatures and humidity swing. Keep key documents in a fireproof, water resistant box and photograph the contents once.

Write a list of where each important paper is, and give a copy to a trusted person.
