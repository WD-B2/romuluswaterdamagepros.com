How to Prevent Water Damage When You Leave Home in Romulus, MI.

Most big water losses happen when no one is home to notice. A few minutes before you leave can prevent a long cleanup.

Call (844) 950-6900

By the Romulus Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 2026.

Why empty houses are at risk.

A leak that would be a mop-up when you are home becomes a flood when no one is there. Water keeps running for days, soaks the structure and may only be found when you return.

Insurers also look closely at losses in vacant homes, so the habits below protect both the house and a future claim.

Before you leave: the checklist.

  1. Shut off the main water valve for trips of more than a day or two.
  2. Replace old rubber washer hoses with braided ones and check for bulges.
  3. Set the thermostat to a safe temperature in cold months, not to off.
  4. Test the sump pump and make sure the pit has no debris.
  5. Clear gutters and downspouts so water flows away from the foundation.
  6. Open cabinet doors on exterior walls during a freeze.

Have someone check in.

Ask a neighbor or relative to check inside every few days, especially in winter or during storm season. Give them the main shutoff location and our number, and tell them to call if they see water or hear running water.

Smart leak sensors under sinks, near the water heater and by the sump pit alert your phone if water appears, and they are inexpensive compared with a cleanup.

Vacation homes and long absences.

For a month or longer, close the main valve, drain the lines if the house will be cold and ask someone to walk through weekly. Document the condition of the house before you leave with a short video, which also helps if you ever need to file a claim.

Tell your insurer if the home will be vacant for a long stretch, because many policies change what they cover after a set number of days.

What to tell the person checking the house.

Give them a short written note. It should say where the main shutoff is, where the sump pump and water heater are, who to call in an emergency and what you want them to do if they find water, which is to close the valve first and then call.

Remind them not to step into standing water near outlets, and to photograph anything they see before moving it.

Cheap upgrades that pay off.

A few inexpensive changes reduce risk for years. Braided steel supply hoses, a water heater drip pan with a drain, battery powered leak sensors and an automatic shutoff valve on the main line all cost far less than a flooded floor.

An automatic shutoff valve closes when it senses unusual flow, which can stop a burst while you are asleep or on the road. Ask a plumber whether one suits your system.

The industry standard behind prevention advice.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration is the published industry reference, and its water categories explain why a small leak left for a week becomes a bigger loss. Time is one of the main factors it names.

We cite the standard to explain why prevention checks matter. It is an industry document and not a claim of certification.

Romulus note: local weather facts.

The National Weather Service 1991 to 2020 climate normals for Detroit show about 34 inches of precipitation and 45 inches of snow a year, which means freezing and thawing every winter. Those two facts are why a heated house and a working sump pump matter, and why a power outage plan, such as a battery backup for the pump, is worth thinking about before you travel.

If you come home to water.

Stop the source if it is safe, keep power off in wet areas unless you can reach the panel dry and photograph the damage. Then call the 24/7 line, and see our pages on burst pipe cleanup and frozen pipe damage for what happens next.
