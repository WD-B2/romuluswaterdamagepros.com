Selling a House With Water Damage in Romulus, MI: Disclosure Basics.

Past water problems do not end a sale, but hiding them can. Here is how disclosure works and what to do before you list.

Call (844) 950-6900

By the Romulus Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 2026.

What Michigan asks sellers about water.

Michigan has a Seller Disclosure Act, and its statutory form is titled the Seller's Disclosure Statement. It asks about the basement and crawl space, including whether there has been evidence of water, and about settling, flooding, drainage, structural or grading problems.

It also asks about major damage from fire, wind, floods or landslides, and whether you have flood insurance on the property. This is general information, not legal advice, so ask a real estate attorney about your specific situation.

Repair before you list, or sell as is.

If the damage is active or recent, buyers and lenders will want to see it fixed. Dry the structure, repair what was damaged and keep documentation, since a clean, documented repair is easier to explain than an open problem.

Selling as is can make sense for a larger problem, but expect lower offers and more questions from inspectors. Ask your agent which route fits your market.

Documentation buyers like to see.

  • Photos before and after repair.
  • Moisture readings showing the structure was dry.
  • Invoices for drying and repair.
  • Permits and inspection records where work required them.
  • Notes from the plumber or contractor who fixed the cause.

Pricing a house with a repaired leak.

A repaired and documented leak usually hurts a sale less than an unexplained stain. Buyers worry most about the unknown, so show them what happened, what was done and how you know it is dry. Keep the drying records from any water extraction work, because a buyer may ask for them.

If a repair is incomplete, get a written quote and offer it to the buyer, or adjust the price. Your agent can tell you how similar homes have sold in the area.

Staging and showing a house after a loss.

Fresh paint over a repaired wall is fine, but only after the structure is dry and the cause is fixed. Painting over damp drywall or an active stain can create a bigger problem and raises suspicion.

Keep fans and dehumidifiers out of sight if possible, and keep a folder of records on the counter so buyers can see the work. Honest, organized answers build trust.

Insurance claims and a future buyer.

Some buyers and lenders ask about prior insurance claims. A claim history is one more reason to keep clear records, including what caused the loss and how it was repaired.

If you filed a claim and did not complete repairs, talk to your agent before you list, because open claims can complicate a sale.

Talking to your agent and your attorney.

Your agent knows how local buyers react to a repaired leak, and an attorney can tell you how the disclosure rules apply to your facts. Bring your records to both conversations.

Answer questions on the disclosure form based on what you know, and update the form if you learn something new before closing.

The standard buyers may ask about.

A buyer or inspector may ask whether drying followed the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, the published reference for professional water damage restoration. Records such as moisture readings and photos are how that question gets answered.

The standard is an industry document and not a certificate. Ask for the records at the time of the work and keep them with your house papers.

Working with inspectors.

A home inspector will check basements, ceilings and walls for stains and moisture. If you have repaired damage, have the documentation ready so the inspector can see the full picture.

If an inspection finds a possible leak, get it checked rather than arguing. A moisture meter reading and a plumber or contractor report can settle the question, and our basement cleanup page explains what drying looks like.
