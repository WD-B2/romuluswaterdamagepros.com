What we do.

Water damage restoration in Romulus, MI, from the first call to the last repair.

Water damage restoration is the work of removing water from a building, drying every material it soaked, cleaning what can be saved and repairing what cannot. In Romulus that can mean a split supply line in a ranch off Wayne Road or a flooded basement after a summer cloudburst. Our 24/7 emergency line is where all of it starts.

Restoration and repair are two halves of one job. Restoration is the emergency half, with water extraction, drying equipment and moisture readings. Repair is the rebuild half, which replaces drywall, flooring, trim and paint once the structure tests dry.

Every call begins with a short conversation about where the water came from, how far it has spread and whether anyone is at risk. We explain the scope and the price before work starts, so you know the plan before a crew touches anything.

The process.

How an emergency call turns into a dry house.

  1. You call the 24/7 line.

    A person asks where the water started, how much there is and whether power or sewage is involved. You get safety steps for the minutes before we arrive.

  2. We scope the loss.

    We check moisture with meters and thermal imaging, then explain what needs to come out, what can dry in place and what it will cost.

  3. Extraction and drying.

    Extractors remove standing water. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the structure while daily readings track progress.

  4. Repair and rebuild.

    Once readings reach drying goals we replace drywall, insulation, flooring and finishes so the room is usable again.

Repair.

Water damage repair once the structure is dry.

Repair begins only after the building tests dry, because closing a wall over wet framing just hides the problem. A moisture meter reading on the studs and subfloor tells us when the structure is ready. Many professionals follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration when they set drying goals and classify water, and we use it as a reference too.

The rebuild itself is ordinary construction work. Wet drywall and insulation come out in measured cuts, new board goes in, and flooring, trim and paint follow. We scope each of those steps in writing, so repair does not turn into a second surprise bill.

Some homeowners want only the water removed and drying done, then hire their own contractor for the rebuild. That is a fair way to work, and we will write the drying documentation so another contractor can pick up from there.

Local conditions.

What water losses in Romulus tend to look like.

On July 28, 2025 the National Weather Service in Detroit recorded 3.31 inches of rain in Romulus in about an hour and a half, with flooded roads, stranded vehicles and downed power lines across the area. A downpour like that can overload floor drains and window wells anywhere in the city, so check your lowest level first and call if you find water.

It was not the first time. In August 2014 the weather service reported 4 to 6 inches of rain in about four hours across Wayne, southern Oakland and Macomb counties. Events like these are why we wrote the storm water damage page and the flood damage page first.

Winter brings a different pattern. The National Weather Service 1991 to 2020 normals for Detroit show about 34 inches of precipitation and 45 inches of snow a year, so freeze and thaw cycles reach the plumbing every season. A pipe that splits behind a wall is a burst pipe job, and one that thawed after a cold snap is covered on the frozen pipe page.

Romulus covers about 36 square miles, with 25,178 residents and 10,280 housing units at the 2020 census, plus Detroit Metropolitan Airport and the I-94 and I-275 corridors.
