Floodwater in the house: what to do now.

Floodwater is an emergency, and our 24/7 line is open for it. Stay out of standing water that may touch outlets or appliances, keep power off in flooded areas unless you can reach the panel dry, and call your utility if you smell gas or see sparks.

Do not wade through it if you can avoid it, because floodwater can carry soil, sewage and chemicals from outside. Move to a dry level, photograph what you can safely see and call.

Big storms flood many homes at once and nights are no exception. Same-day response is what we work toward when crews are available, and we will be honest about timing on the phone.

Romulus has seen this before.

Flood cleanup, from first look to dry structure.

  1. Assess the hazards.

    We check electrical risk, water depth and likely contamination before anyone enters.

  2. Remove the water.

    Pumps and extractors take out the bulk, then wet vacuums handle edges and stairs.

  3. Haul out what is ruined.

    Soaked carpet, pad, insulation and drywall that cannot be cleaned are bagged and removed.

  4. Clean and dry.

    Surfaces are cleaned with an antimicrobial, then air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure with logged readings.

Flood insurance is a separate question.

The Michigan Department of Insurance and Financial Services says flood damage is not covered under a standard homeowners policy. Flood coverage may come from the National Flood Insurance Program or a private insurer, and limited water or sewer back-up coverage can be added by endorsement.

When you call your insurer, DIFS suggests describing the loss as water damage rather than flood, because other causes such as a failed sump pump or sewer backup may be covered. Photograph everything and keep damaged items until the insurer tells you otherwise.

What to document before cleanup.

Take photos or video of every wet room, including water lines on walls, damaged belongings and the outside of the house. Photograph serial numbers on appliances that were in water.

Keep damaged items until your insurer says you can discard them, unless they are a safety risk. A written list of what was lost helps your claim.

What affects the cost of flood cleanup.

Cost factors.

Depth, area, contamination and how long the water sat set the scope. Flood jobs tend to include more removal and disposal than clean-water jobs because contaminated porous materials do not go back in.
