Water Damage in a Rental in Romulus, MI: What Tenants and Landlords Should Know.

When a rental floods, both sides need a plan. Here is who calls whom and what to document.

Call (844) 950-6900

By the Romulus Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 2026.

First steps for a tenant.

If you rent and water starts to spread, stop the source if you can do it safely, keep out of wet areas near power and call your landlord or property manager right away. Then document the damage with photos and video.

Put the report in writing, such as an email or a text, so there is a record of when you told them. Keep copies of everything.

First steps for a landlord.

A landlord should respond quickly, shut off the source, arrange extraction and drying and keep the tenant informed. Notify your insurer and keep records of what you did and when.

If a tenant's belongings were damaged, they should call their own renters insurer. Landlord policies usually cover the building and not the tenant's contents.

What the Michigan statute says.

Michigan law, at MCL 554.139, says a landlord covenants that the premises and common areas are fit for the intended use and that the landlord will keep the premises in reasonable repair during the lease. It also requires compliance with applicable health and safety laws, with an exception where the tenant caused the problem through willful or irresponsible conduct or failure to act.

That means a landlord has a duty to repair a leak that makes a unit unfit, but facts matter. Ask a tenant rights organization or an attorney about your situation, because this is general information and not legal advice.

Documenting for both sides.

  • Photos and video of damage before anything is moved.
  • Dated messages about when the problem was reported.
  • Names of anyone who inspected the unit.
  • Receipts for emergency costs and lodging.
  • A list of damaged belongings with approximate values.

If the landlord does not respond.

Send a second written notice and keep the first. If the unit is unsafe or the leak keeps spreading, ask your city about inspection options and consider contacting a tenant rights organization or an attorney.

Take photos on each visit, and note any new damage. Do not withhold rent or make major repairs on your own without advice, because the rules can be complicated and the consequences serious.

Moving out, temporary housing and deposits.

If the unit cannot be lived in during repairs, ask in writing about temporary housing, rent adjustments and how long the work is expected to take. Renters insurance may cover additional living costs, so check your policy.

Document the condition of the unit when you leave, including photos of any repaired areas, so a deposit dispute does not follow a water loss.

The standard both sides can point to.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the published industry reference for professional water damage restoration. Tenants and landlords can both use its terms, such as water category, to describe what happened.

It is a shared vocabulary and not a certificate. Written notice and photos still come first.

Communication tips that avoid fights.

Keep messages short and factual, say what you saw and when, and ask what the next step is and by when. If you do not get a reply, follow up in writing and keep the thread.

If you are a landlord, tell your tenant what is happening and give an honest timeline. People take disruption better when they know what to expect.
