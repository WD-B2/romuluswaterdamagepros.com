Winter, thaw and Garden City plumbing.

Garden City sees the same freeze and thaw cycles as the rest of Wayne County, and the National Weather Service 1991 to 2020 normals for Detroit show about 45 inches of snow and 34 inches of precipitation a year. Pipes in garages and exterior walls are the first to give out in a cold snap.

A city that became a village in 1927 and a city in 1933 has plenty of older homes. That tends to mean older water heaters, older supply lines and older appliance hookups, which is why leaks from those sources are what Garden City callers ask us about most.

Emergency water damage in Garden City.

A running leak is an emergency, and the 24/7 line is open for Garden City callers at any hour. Tell us your street and where the water is, and we will explain what to do while the next steps are arranged.

Same-day response is what we work toward when crews are available, including nights and weekends. We will be honest about timing when winter storms keep crews busy.

If water is still spreading.

Shut it off.

Close the water heater supply, the appliance valve or the main, whichever stops the flow.

Be careful with power.

Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel dry, and do not touch a wet water heater.

Move and photograph.

Lift valuables, pull small rugs and photograph the damage for your insurer.

Around Garden City: neighborhoods and landmarks.

Garden City grew in the old Nankin Township, and it is known as the home of the first Kmart, opened in 1962, and the first Little Caesars, opened in 1959. The Folker Building served as the first city hall, and Henry Ford's honeymoon cottage was moved here in 1952.

The city has about 27,000 residents in ZIP codes 48135 and 48136, and the Middle Rouge River runs nearby, so check the FEMA flood map if you are close to the river. We work from Romulus, about nine miles southwest, and will tell you honestly how long reaching you is likely to take.

Documenting the loss for your insurer.

Photograph the failed part, the label on the water heater and the wet floors before cleanup. Keep damaged items until your insurer says otherwise, and tell your insurer the loss is water damage.

Many policies treat a sudden failure differently from long term seepage, so ask what applies. We provide a written scope, photos and readings for your adjuster.

The industry standard behind our approach in Garden City.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the published reference for professional water damage restoration. In Garden City, where older water heaters and supply lines are common causes, it guides how we classify the water and set drying goals.

We cite it as an industry document. It is not a claim that we hold a certificate.
