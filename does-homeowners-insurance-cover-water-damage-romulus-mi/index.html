Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage in Romulus, MI?

Whether your policy pays depends on where the water came from, not just that your home got wet. Here is how to read it.

Call (844) 950-6900

By the Romulus Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 2026.

The short answer: it depends on the cause.

Most homeowners ask one question after a leak: will my insurance pay for this? The honest answer is that it depends on your policy and on the cause of the water, which is why two neighbors with similar damage can get different results.

The table below shows how the main causes are commonly treated. It is a general guide, not a promise, and your own policy wording controls.

Words in a policy that decide a claim.

Four ideas come up again and again. Sudden and accidental describes a burst or a failure that happens at a point in time, and gradual seepage or long term leaking is often excluded. Surface water and flood exclusions leave out water that comes in from outside.

Endorsements are add-ons that change what is covered, and sewer or water back-up is a common one. Your deductible is the amount you pay before the policy pays, and for a small loss it can be larger than the damage.

Reading those four sections of your policy before you have a claim saves a great deal of confusion later.

What to do before you call your insurer.

  1. Stop the water and protect the property from further damage, since most policies expect that.
  2. Photograph and video the damage before anything is moved.
  3. Keep damaged items until your insurer tells you to discard them.
  4. Have your policy number ready and call the claims line or your agent.
  5. Describe the loss as water damage and ask what coverage applies, rather than naming a cause you are unsure of.

Where restoration fits in.

A restoration company can document the loss with photos, moisture readings and a written scope, and it can start extraction and drying that your policy usually expects you to do promptly. It cannot decide coverage, and it should never tell you a claim will be paid.

If you are wondering which cause fits your situation, the pages on burst pipes and basement water describe the usual patterns, and the call line can help you think through what to tell your agent.

A worked example: three leaks, three different outcomes.

Picture three Romulus homes with similar wet floors. In the first, a supply line to a toilet splits on a Saturday and floods the bathroom, which is a sudden and accidental event that many policies cover subject to the deductible.

In the second, a slow drip under a kitchen sink soaks the cabinet floor for months before anyone notices, and the policy may treat that as gradual seepage or a maintenance issue. In the third, a heavy rain pushes water through a basement window well, which is surface water and is not covered by a standard homeowners policy.

The damage in all three looks alike, but the outcomes differ because the cause differs. That is why describing the loss accurately and asking what applies matters more than guessing.

Small losses, deductibles and your premium.

If the damage is close to your deductible, filing may not pay off, and some homeowners choose to pay for a small repair themselves. Ask your agent how water claims are handled on your policy and whether a claim affects renewal.

You do not have to decide alone or in a hurry. Document the damage first, because photos and readings keep your options open whichever way you go.

Romulus note: what Michigan's insurance regulator says.

Romulus note: primary source facts.

The Michigan Department of Insurance and Financial Services states that flood damage is not covered under a standard homeowners policy and that flood coverage may be available through the National Flood Insurance Program or a private insurer. It also says homeowners may buy endorsements that offer limited coverage for water or sewer back-up.

The same guidance advises telling your insurer you have water damage and asking what applies, instead of saying it was a flood, because other causes such as a sewer backup or failing sump pump may be covered. Questions to ask your agent this week.

Do I have water or sewer back-up coverage, and what is the limit? Is sump pump failure covered? How does my policy treat a pipe that freezes? What is my deductible for water losses?

Homeowners in Belleville and Inkster, where lakes and rivers sit close to neighborhoods, should also ask whether flood coverage is worth adding. Ask before the storm, because most flood policies have a waiting period.
