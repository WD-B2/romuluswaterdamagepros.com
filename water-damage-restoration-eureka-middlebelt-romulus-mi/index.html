The Eureka and Middlebelt area of Romulus.

Eureka Road and Middlebelt Road meet in the middle of Romulus, and the area around that crossing is one of the places the city has pointed to when talking about flood maps. Middlebelt also runs up to the I-94 interchange, which makes the area easy to reach from the freeway.

We describe this as an area around two roads and not as a neighborhood with official boundaries, because no source we found draws one. If you are unsure whether your street counts, call and give the address.

Our base is on Wayne Road in the same city, so crews cover this area as part of Romulus coverage.

Why flood maps matter here.

In 2012 Romulus officials challenged a FEMA flood map covering about 1,000 parcels, calling it outdated. The city paid for a hydraulic study that, according to a Detroit Free Press report, indicated about 40 percent of those parcels should not have been mapped as floodplain.

That report named the Eureka and Middlebelt area, floodplain along the North Branch Ecorse Creek Drain, Hale Creek and the Carter Drain, and the McClaughrey Drain at Hannan and Ecorse roads. Maps change over time, so neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified here.

What the story means for you is practical. Look up your exact address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, ask your insurer what your policy covers and do not assume either way.

What to do if water is still spreading.

Stop the source.

Close the main valve or the fixture shutoff if it is safe. For rain or rising groundwater there is nothing to shut off, so go to the next step.

Mind the power.

Keep power off in wet areas only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. Stay out of any room where water touches outlets.

Lift and photograph.

Move valuables and furniture legs out of the water and photograph the damage for your insurance claim.

Insurance and flood coverage.

The Michigan Department of Insurance and Financial Services says flood damage is not covered under a standard homeowners policy and that flood coverage may be available through the National Flood Insurance Program or a private insurer. Sump pump failure or sewer backup may be covered by an endorsement, so check your policy.

Tell your insurer you have water damage, photograph or video everything and keep damaged items until the insurer says otherwise. We provide a written scope and moisture readings for your adjuster.

Storms in Romulus.

On July 28, 2025 the National Weather Service recorded 3.31 inches of rain in Romulus in about an hour and a half, with flooded roads and stranded vehicles. That is a citywide record and not a statement about any single street.

After a storm like that, many homes call at once, so the quickest help is to follow the first steps above and photograph the damage while you wait.
